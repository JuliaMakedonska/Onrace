# Jobs to be Done

Built from `personas.md` and `research.md` only. Every job below is followed by the persona it grew from and the specific `research.md` section it traces to. Jobs with no direct backing are moved to **Hypotheses** at the end, not mixed into the main list, per the source documents' own honesty standard (`research.md` line 3, `personas.md` "Honest gaps").

None of the phrasings below name a feature (no "map," "filter," "archive," "source-link field") — only the person's situation, motivation, and desired outcome.

## Why two main jobs, not one main + related

Onrace does two things that don't reduce to one another: **finding races** (triggered by planning ahead — a season, a next event) and **storing/archiving official results** (triggered by the opposite moment — after competing, usually driven by a future need to prove a result). That difference in trigger — one sourced to the discovery-fragmentation evidence, the other to the Proof-of-result findings — is the sole justification for treating them as two co-equal jobs rather than one main job with the other subordinate to it.

*(Audit note: an earlier draft of this section added "an athlete can want one without ever wanting the other in the same sitting" as a second justification. That sentence has been removed — it asserted something about usage timing/co-occurrence that no research.md finding supports, invented rather than sourced. The different-triggers argument above stands on its own and doesn't need it.)*

The earlier single-main-job structure came from an evidence asymmetry, not a real ranking: the discovery job currently has denser, more varied sourcing in `research.md` (four independent sections — see Main Job 1 below) than the archiving job does (one finding, cited three times — see Main Job 2 below). That's a difference in *how much the research documents each job*, not evidence that one job matters less to the person or is subordinate to the other. So they're kept as two co-equal main jobs, with that sourcing gap noted honestly under each rather than used to rank them.

## Main Job 1 — Discovery

> **When** I'm looking for hybrid races to enter, **I want to** see the full range of options — across countries, formats, and dates — instead of checking each source separately, **so that** I can compare them in one place. **[?]** *(the "instead of manually comparing across sources" outcome is an inference — see below — not a directly confirmed user behavior)*

**Sourcing:**
- **Race data availability** states the fragmentation almost verbatim: *"no single source covers HYROX + DEKA + marathons + triathlons + ultras together."*
- **COMPETITORS matrix** gives per-competitor partial-coverage facts: fitnessracingcalendar.com confirmed as "UK and US, with more countries coming soon"; RoxConnect claims "+80 countries" but is HYROX-only; ocrbase covers a broader format set (OCR/trail/rucking) but isn't hybrid-scoped; DUV is ultra-only, single-sport; Athlinks has no discovery/browse UI at all.
- **Three differences #2**: "race discovery is solved via at least four different product shapes... none matches Onrace's planned map-first, cross-format approach."
- **PATTERNS** section: global scope from day one, geography as a primary decision axis for both recreational and competitive athletes.

- **Confirmed:** no single reviewed source (product or data source) covers the full country/format range hybrid athletes might care about — this is stated directly and repeatedly, not inferred from adjacent facts.
- **Still an inference, `[?]`:** that a real athlete personally experiences this as "manually comparing across scattered sources." The closest evidence is the *research team's own* experience manually curating from hyrox.com, spartan.com/en/deka, ocrbase.com, DUV, and marathon majors while seeding data — that's the researchers hitting fragmentation, not an observed athlete doing the same while searching.

## Hypotheses (not backed by research.md — do not treat as validated)

1. > **When** my past results are spread across different timing companies' sites, **I want to** gather them into one personal place, **so that** I'm not hunting through old bookmarks or emails when I need one.

   **Why it's a hypothesis:** the "scattered across timing sites" framing comes from `../CLAUDE.md`, not `research.md`. `research.md` only shows that Boston accepts proof from *any* of several official source types — it never documents an individual athlete's fragmentation problem. Flagged the same way in `personas.md` (Qualifying-Time Archivist → Pains).

2. > **When** I'm asked for a source link while logging a result, **I want** it to take almost no extra effort, **so that** I don't give up partway through submitting.

   **Why it's a hypothesis:** grounded in `research.md` CONCLUSIONS gap 1, which the document itself explicitly labels unvalidated: "None of these are validated with real user/usage data yet."

3. > **When** I don't yet have a fixed way of tracking races, **I want** something that already fits how I plan a season, **so that** I don't have to change my habits to use it.

   **Why it's a hypothesis:** `research.md`'s own "Questions to answer" section poses this and explicitly leaves it open: "where do athletes currently track races/results today — HYROX's own site, Athlinks, DUV, RaceRoster, Strava, spreadsheets?" No answer exists yet.

4. > **When** my self-reported result could be doubted, **I want to** feel confident it won't be dismissed as fake, **so that** logging it feels worthwhile.

   **Why it's a hypothesis:** inferred from the trust-model concerns in `research.md`'s BENCHMARK section (falsification cost, stakes-appropriate rigor) — but this is the researcher's threat model for the product, never a feeling reported by an actual person.

5. > **When** I'm managing everything about being a hybrid athlete — training, watches, registering for races, competing across formats — **I want** one tool that handles all of it, **so that** I stop needing a different tool for every part of my racing life.

   **Why it's a hypothesis:** this was an earlier draft's attempt at a single compound main job, and it overreaches what `research.md` actually supports. The real finding — no competitor combines *discovery + result-archiving* across formats (Three shared market patterns #3; CONCLUSIONS gap 6) — only licenses claims about those two specific jobs (now Main Jobs 1 and 2 above), not about training, wearables, or registration. `../CLAUDE.md` explicitly puts registration/payment handling and training-plan/coaching content out of scope for Onrace, so a "does everything" job is not just unsourced but contradicts the brief's own stated scope.

### Conclusion: 3 jobs for the MVP core

Filtering for "important to the primary persona" *and* "not already covered by the market" — not importance alone, since Related 1 scores a clean 3 for the primary persona but is excluded below because RoxConnect/RoxFit/RoxMatchUp already serve it (just HYROX-only):

1. **Main Job 1 — Discovery.** Clean high score for the primary persona (3) plus the best-evidenced market gap in the whole matrix (four independent `research.md` sections, no single competitor covers the full range). The strongest, least-caveated pick.
2. **Related Job 3 — Log a result with required source proof.** Its importance-for-primary rating is a caveated 2, not a clean 3 (the supporting evidence is general trust-mechanism reasoning, not primary-persona-specific) — worth flagging honestly rather than rounding up. But the market-coverage gap is the sharpest in the table ("No," explicitly "genuinely novel"), and this is the most concrete, feature-shaped instantiation of the archiving job.
3. **Main Job 2 — Results archiving, combined with discovery across formats.** Also a caveated 2 for the primary persona (inferred from competitor product existence, not direct evidence). Included because `research.md`'s own Findings → decisions names this combination explicitly as "Onrace's actual whitespace" — the gap is structural (no one combines both across formats), even though the persona-importance evidence under it is thinner than Main Job 1's.

**Called out, not included:** Related Job 4 (retrieve proof for an elite-race application) is the single strongest job for the *secondary* Qualifying-Time Archivist persona (3, cleanly sourced) and has a real market gap ("Partially" covered at best) — but it scores a sourced **1** for the primary persona, since HYROX Worlds qualification is explicitly placement-based, not proof-of-time. It's a legitimate priority for serving the archivist persona specifically; it just doesn't qualify under an "important to primary" filter as the evidence currently stands.

### Feature-candidates to cut

Checking `../CLAUDE.md`'s `results` table fields against all six jobs in the matrix, two fields didn't map to any of them:

- **`category`** (e.g., age/gender category on a result) — no job above needs this to be captured; no `research.md` finding motivated it.
- **`notes`** (free-text notes on a result) — same: no job in the matrix is served by it, and nothing in `research.md` surfaced a need for freeform annotation.

**Resolved — cut, not kept.** Both fields have been removed from `../CLAUDE.md`'s `results` data model, with a note there: "removed — no job maps to this; reconsider if the archiving job proves more central after validation." Every remaining field in the `races` and `results` tables traces to at least one row in the matrix above.

## Design implication: the archiving *sub-jobs* map to a different persona than Discovery does

Reviewing the matrix above by column rather than by row surfaces something worth calling out explicitly — stated precisely this time, since an earlier draft of this section blurred two different rows together:

- **Discovery (Main Job 1) → Primary persona (The HYROX-First Hybrid Athlete).** Scores a clean 3, with the most independently-sourced evidence in the whole matrix (Sport taxonomy findings, COMPETITORS HARD tier, Race data availability, Three differences #2).
- **Related Jobs 3–4 (logging a result with proof; retrieving it for an elite-race application) → Secondary persona (The Qualifying-Time Archivist).** These score 3 for the Archivist, cleanly sourced to Proof-of-result findings — while the *same* two jobs score only a caveated 2, a sourced 1, or `[?]` for the primary persona (Related Job 4: HYROX Worlds qualification is explicitly placement-based, "no analogous 'proof of time' submission flow exists for HYROX the way it does for Boston").

**Correction from an earlier draft:** this is deliberately *not* phrased as "Main Job 2 maps to the Archivist." Main Job 2's own row (the compound "discovery + archive combined in one product" job) scores **2** for the Primary persona and **`[?]`** — unscored, not lower — for the Archivist. So Main Job 2's own row doesn't actually support handing that job to the Archivist; the persona-handoff evidence lives specifically in Related Jobs 3–4, not in Main Job 2 itself. (Both rows are also subject to the circularity caveat above — the Archivist's 3s here come from the same section she was constructed from.)

This isn't a reason to change which persona is primary — that choice is independently justified by the discovery-side evidence, not by these jobs. But it means the concrete *acts* of logging and retrieving a result shouldn't be designed by asking what the primary persona needs. When wireframing those specific screens, design them around the Qualifying-Time Archivist's situation and motivation (needing to produce official proof of a past time for an elite-race application) rather than forcing them through the HYROX-first athlete's framing. See the matching note added to `personas.md`.
