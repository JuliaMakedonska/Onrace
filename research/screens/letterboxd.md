# Letterboxd — screenshot captured

Captured via Playwright MCP, 2026-09-02: `letterboxd-home.png` (full-page homepage, public, no login wall). Text below verified against the page's accessibility tree.

**Scale, confirmed on-page:** "3,938,638,123 films watched" logged across the platform — a genuinely massive scale for a self-reported logging product, useful context for how far this pattern can go with essentially zero verification.

**"Letterboxd lets you…" — confirmed exact copy:**
- "Keep track of every film you've ever watched (or just start from the day you join)"
- "Show some love for your favorite films, lists and reviews with a 'like'"
- "Write and share reviews, and follow friends and other members to read theirs"
- "Rate each film on a five-star scale (with halves) to record and share your reaction"
- "Keep a diary of your film watching (and upgrade to **Pro** for comprehensive stats)"
- "Compile and share lists of films on any topic and keep a watchlist of films to see"

**Homepage below the fold:** a live activity feed of popular reviews and lists this week (real member reviews with like counts, e.g. thousands of likes on a Grand Theft Auto VI trailer "review"), a "Popular Reviewers" leaderboard-style list, and editorial "Recent Stories" content (festival coverage, staff picks) — a genuinely social, engagement-driven homepage, the opposite of a utility tool.

**Monetization:** freemium — "Pro" is referenced inline (diary/stats) and in the nav alongside Films/Lists/Members/Journal/Apps/Year in Review.

**Relevance to Onrace:** this is the strongest available proof that "a personal archive of things you've experienced, each entry backed by nothing but your own word" can reach massive scale with near-zero verification, precisely because the stakes are low (per the ownership-credibility BENCHMARK section). It's a scale/engagement benchmark, not a mechanism to copy — Onrace's brief explicitly rules out the social feed, likes, and follower graph that make Letterboxd's model work.
