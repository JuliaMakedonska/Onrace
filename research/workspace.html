Onrace

An interactive map of hybrid-athlete races, plus a personal archive of official results — for athletes who need one place to find their next race and prove their last one.

This page is the competitive audit, ownership-credibility benchmark, and race-discovery UX pattern analysis behind the MVP. Compiled from research/research.md — desktop web research plus live browser screenshots, no user interviews yet.

Three shared market patterns

1. Freemium (or a B2B subsidy) is the default business modelConfirmed for RoxConnect (Free / $11.99/mo) and inferred for Athlinks (ChronoTrack footer link). Nobody charges for the core “find/log a race” job itself.
2. Nobody uses a self-reported, required-source-link trust modelAthlinks auto-matches from timing feeds; Strava’s data is GPS-tracked; ocrbase and RoxConnect carry no verification layer at all. Onrace’s “the link is the evidence” approach is genuinely novel among this set.
3. Discovery and archiving are usually separate — with two single-sport exceptionsRoxConnect discovers partners, ocrbase discovers races only, Athlinks archives results only, Strava bolted discovery onto an existing activity-tracking product. But RoxFit and RoxMatchUp do combine both halves — scoped entirely to HYROX. No competitor combines both halves across formats the way Onrace plans to.

Three differences

1. Scale and maturity vary enormouslyAthlinks (Life Time-linked) and Strava (established platform) versus ocrbase’s and RoxConnect’s small-team footprint. Onrace starts at the small end of this spectrum.
2. Four different product shapes solve “race discovery”Pure calendar browsing (ocrbase), partner-matching (RoxConnect), filters + a personalized rail on a social app (Strava), a claimed-results database with no discovery layer (Athlinks). None matches Onrace’s planned map-first, cross-format approach.
3. Revenue structure differs by categoryDirect consumer subscription (RoxConnect) vs. B2B-subsidizes-free-product (Athlinks) vs. no visible monetization (ocrbase). CLAUDE.md’s “no monetization plan” puts Onrace in the same bucket as the least-resourced competitor here.

Benchmark

Ownership-credibility benchmark

Which lightweight mechanisms should Onrace’s MVP borrow to make self-reported result ownership credible, given there’s no manual verification team? Benchmarked against 5 products that handle self-reported/unverified ownership claims well in their own niche, scored 1–5 on 8 criteria.

Score anchors — what a 1 and a 5 look like

  • Identity persistence — 1 = anonymous/throwaway identity, free to discard and remake; 5 = tied to a durable, hard-to-fake real or professional identity.
  • Falsification cost — 1 = as easy to fake as typing a sentence; 5 = faking it takes roughly as much effort as actually doing the real thing.
  • Independent cross-reference — 1 = no external record exists to check the claim against; 5 = a public, independently-run database anyone can search to confirm it.
  • Peer/social visibility — 1 = no one else ever sees the claim; 5 = surfaced directly to people with the specific knowledge to catch a lie.
  • Automatable plausibility checks — 1 = any input is accepted, no rule-based check at all; 5 = obviously implausible claims are auto-rejected with no human reviewer needed.
  • Stakes-appropriate rigor — 1 = verification effort is badly mismatched to what a false claim would gain someone; 5 = the built-in trust-signal is well-calibrated to the actual stakes of lying.
  • Dispute/correction mechanism — 1 = no way to flag or fix a wrong/fraudulent entry after the fact; 5 = a lightweight, self-serve way to correct or contest a claim.
  • Source-artifact requirement — 1 = a free-text claim is accepted on its own, nothing else required; 5 = an external, independently-hosted artifact (a link or document) is mandatory before the claim is accepted.

Top 3 mechanisms for Onrace’s MVP

1. Make the source link mandatory, not optional, at submissionLinkedIn scores 5/5 on identity persistence but only 3/5 on source-artifact requirement — its Credential URL field is optional, and most users skip a step that isn’t required. Onrace’s brief already plans a required link; this is direct evidence not to soften that in implementation.
2. Label every result “self-reported + source-linked,” never “verified”Strava wins the table (23/40) largely via structural segregation, not verification — manual entries are excluded from leaderboards by rule. Always show “via [domain of official_result_url],” never a checkmark implying Onrace vouches for it.
3. Add automatable plausibility checks at submission timeThe one criterion every product scored weakly on (1–3/5) — a cheap opportunity to do better than all five references at once: reject dead links, flag implausible finish times, flag exact duplicate race+time submissions.

One mechanism that won’t work

Peer / social visibility Letterboxd (3/5) and Strava (4/5) both lean on a critical mass of peers who know the claimant’s real skill level to organically catch false claims. Onrace’s results archive is explicitly not a social feed (no following, comments, or public sharing of results in MVP, per CLAUDE.md). With no followers and a thin launch user base, there’s no audience positioned to notice or contest a fabricated result — mechanisms 1–3 above have to substitute for the social layer this pattern actually depends on.
Patterns

Race-discovery interaction pattern

Chosen pattern: map-first spatial exploration, with faceted filtering (region, date, sport type) layered on top as its companion, not a separate feature.

This isn’t a speculative pick — it’s already decided in CLAUDE.md: MVP feature 1 reads “Interactive map of races — filterable by region, date, and sport type,” and the tech stack section has already locked in Leaflet + OpenStreetMap as the map layer.

Why it fits

1. It’s the documented plan, not a competing optionCLAUDE.md names the map explicitly as MVP feature 1, and the Leaflet + OSM tech-stack decision is already made, not open.
2. The audience is geography-driven by designGlobal scope from day one; both recreational athletes (“what can I travel to”) and competitive athletes (multi-region racing calendars) make geography a primary decision axis — matching ocrbase’s and AllTrails’ location-based browsing for the same “choose one of many” task.
3. The scale-breakdown risk doesn’t apply yet — and there’s now a concrete threshold to watchMVP data is hand-seeded via SQL — realistically tens to low hundreds of races, the same range where ocrbase’s and AllTrails’ maps stay legible without clustering logic. IRONMAN’s own “All Races” map needs clustering at 161 races globally — real evidence that low hundreds is roughly where clustering stops being optional, not just a theoretical ceiling.
Against the alternatives: faceted filter + browse alone is the closest runner-up — CLAUDE.md’s own feature line already licenses it as the map’s companion for an athlete who already knows their date/sport constraints. A personalized recommendation feed doesn’t fit: it needs behavioral history Onrace won’t have at a cold-start launch, and no ranking pipeline is budgeted in the Supabase-only stack.
Conclusions

Open gaps & hypotheses

For each open gap surfaced by the analysis above, a hypothesis and the section it follows from. None of these are validated with real user/usage data yet.

1No competitor requires a mandatory source link

Gap — Will a required link cause submission drop-off?

Hypothesis — Friction will be measurable but survivable: the audience already produces official result links for other purposes (e.g. Boston Marathon qualifying-time proof), so the extra step is small relative to existing motivation — but net effect is likely fewer results logged per user, not zero adoption.

Follows from: Competitors
2HYROX-specific competitors' monetization is more varied than first assumed

Gap — RoxConnect confirmed subscription (~$11.99/mo), but a closer look shows RoxFit is a free app with an unpriced "Ultra" tier still in waitlist and RoxMatchUp is free with microtransactions (RoxCoins), not a subscription — while Onrace's brief states no monetization plan at all.

Hypothesis — "No monetization" is workable through MVP validation but not indefinitely — if revenue is needed later, the realistic options are a direct consumer model (subscription like RoxConnect, or micro-transactions like RoxMatchUp) or a B2B/organizer-subsidy model (like Athlinks×ChronoTrack) — there's no single obvious category default to fall back on, so this is a genuinely open design choice, not a foregone one.

Follows from: Competitors
3Could Strava expand into HYROX/DEKA?

Gap — Would that turn a SOFT competitor into a HARD one?

Hypothesis — Low probability inside Onrace’s 2026-11-01 MVP window — every race sampled in the logged-in screenshots was running-only, and expansion would need a new data partnership, not just a UI change. Data not confirmed beyond this one screenshot pass — worth a periodic recheck, not a blocking risk.

Follows from: Competitors
4Are automated checks enough without peer visibility?

Gap — Is automatable plausibility-checking a sufficient substitute for the ruled-out peer-visibility mechanism?

Hypothesis — Sufficient to catch crude fabrications (impossible times, dead links, exact duplicates) but not a real-but-mismatched link — an acceptable residual risk given Onrace’s trust model is explicitly “the link is the evidence,” not “Onrace verifies.” A known, deliberate limit, not a hidden gap.

Follows from: Benchmark
5Map-UI implementation choice not yet decided

Gap — List/Map toggle vs. AllTrails-style split-pane, and no clustering plan for catalog growth.

Hypothesis — Toggle-first (ocrbase’s pattern) is the right MVP choice given the 2-month timeline and small seed catalog, with split-pane and clustering deferred to a v2 once real usage — not assumption — shows whether users hit that friction. Data not confirmed against Onrace’s actual eventual catalog size.

Follows from: Patterns
6RoxFit and RoxMatchUp already combine discovery + results

Gap — Both already combine race discovery and a personal results/leaderboard view in one HYROX-specific app — is the map+archive combination still genuine whitespace for Onrace?

Hypothesis — Yes, but narrower than first stated — both existing combinations are single-sport (HYROX only); no competitor reviewed across all 15 combines discovery and archive across HYROX + DEKA + marathons + triathlons + ultras together. Onrace's differentiation claim should be restated as cross-format breadth combined with the archive, not the discovery+archive combination alone, which two competitors already have at smaller scope.

Follows from: Competitors

That's a difference in how much the research documents each job, not in which one matters more.
